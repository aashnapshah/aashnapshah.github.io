Aashna P. Shah, PhD

Berkowitz Postdoctoral Research Fellow · Harvard Medical School

I am a Berkowitz Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Department of Biomedical Informatics at Harvard Medical School, where I work with Arjun (Raj) Manrai. I completed my PhD in Systems, Synthetic, and Quantitative Biology at Harvard University in 2026, co-advised by Raj Manrai and Chirag Patel.

My research asks what "normal" means in clinical medicine. Reference ranges, diagnostic thresholds, and risk scores are built from population averages, and I study when those averages fail individual patients, how to personalize them without losing clinical signal, and what happens when the task is handed to AI. I work with large-scale longitudinal health records, medical images, and clinical text, and I care about building methods that hold up in real deployment settings.

Research

Learning Representations of Patient Baselines

Population reference intervals miss patients whose values drift within the "normal" range, while purely personal baselines flag healthy variation. I build sequence models over longitudinal laboratory histories that condition on both a patient's own trajectory and population priors, to detect disease earlier and estimate risk more precisely.

Fairness and Robustness in Clinical Algorithms

Many clinical equations still adjust for race, and foundation models can look accurate while relying on prompt wording, acquisition protocol, or patient demographics. I develop methods that identify the individual-level measurements behind demographic corrections, and I stress-test vision-language and imaging models across subgroups and prompts to expose the shortcuts they learn.

Agentic Systems for Clinical Reasoning and Evaluation

Multi-agent systems that recommend laboratory tests and support diagnostic reasoning, evaluated on Clalit Health Services, MIMIC-IV, and NEJM clinicopathological conference cases, together with scalable methods for judging medical AI outputs against physician judgment.
